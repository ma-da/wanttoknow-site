COVID has dominated our lives, conversations, and our media systems for the last several years. Meanwhile, the culture and information wars about COVID issues have generated ferocious argument, yet very little clarity about how we can engage with each other across the vast differences in the news we consume.

The bedrock of democracy is the open flow of information and honest debate, which includes exploring what's left out of any single narrative or given piece of information. This is especially true for COVID issues. On one hand, the official COVID narrative promotes lockdown policies, vaccine mandates, and trusting our government agencies as the main source of information. On the other hand, a growing number of people are raising concerns about the devastating impacts of lockdown policies, the complex web of flawed studies and data integrity issues, deeper political agendas, lack of transparency, blatant censorship, and untold stories of vaccine injuries that are much more common than we might think. Now that some time has passed, many COVID-related concerns once discredited are no longer considered a “conspiracy theory.”

For example, are COVID vaccine injuries rare? The Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) is a voluntary government reporting system that only captures a portion of the actual injuries. Vaccine adverse event numbers are made publically available, and currently show 1,630,913 COVID vaccine injury reports, 37,231 COVID Vaccine Reported Deaths, and 214,906 COVID Vaccine Reported Hospitalizations.

If accepting the official COVID story was indeed the most effective way to end the pandemic, a democratic and pro-science society would still seek to understand alternative perspectives pointing to harm and injustice. Many of these harms are only beginning to come to light.
